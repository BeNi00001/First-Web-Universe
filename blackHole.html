THE BLACK HOLE

A black hole is a place where gravity has won. So much material is packed into such a small space that nothing can escape — not even light. That is why we cannot see one directly. The edge around it is called the event horizon : cross it, and there is no way back.
Most form when a very large star runs out of fuel and collapses under its own weight. And despite the myth, they do not suck things in. If our Sun were swapped for a black hole of the same mass, Earth would keep orbiting exactly as it does now — just very cold, and very dark.
#Source of the information for this page: Black Hole Basics.

  • The black hole at the centre of the galaxy M87 weighs more than 6 billion times as much as our Sun.
  • When two black holes crash into each other, they shake space itself. We can detect those ripples from Earth — they are called gravitational waves.
  • Time runs slower near a black hole. If you watched a friend fall towards one, you would see them freeze and fade at the edge — while for them, nothing strange would happen at all.
